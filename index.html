Motivation Developmental explorer The childhood surge Relevance

One of the questions animating the lab is why the risk of inflammatory brain injury in adrenoleukodystrophy lines up so tightly with childhood. In the first years of life a young brain spends a large share of the body's energy building myelin, and the lab studies this narrow window as a time of metabolic vulnerability, the years when disorders like ALD and MS tend to set off demyelinating lesions.

I built a small tool over a public map of the developing human brain so you can look up any gene and see how its level moves from early fetal life to adulthood, across 524 brain samples.

How it works

Each gene was measured in brain tissue at ages from 8 weeks after conception out to 40 years. Type a gene below and the chart plots its level along that timeline, one point per age, with birth marked and the early-childhood window shaded, so you can see whether it surges during the years when ALD tends to strike.

The early-childhood surge is dominated by the machinery of myelin, the lipids it is built from, and the peroxisomal handling of very long chain fats. This is the same metabolic load the lab points to when it asks why ALD ignites in exactly these years, and why its lesions look so much like the ones in MS.

Why this matters for the lab

If the risk window for ALD is set by a metabolic peak, then the genes that define that peak are worth knowing one by one. A developmental map lets you take any candidate, a peroxisomal transporter, a lipid enzyme, an immune marker, and ask a single question: does it rise during the childhood window when the disease strikes. The same view extends naturally to the lab's work comparing ALD and MS lesions and to its interest in how immune demand competes with the growing brain.

What I would bring

My background is in computational neuroscience and high-dimensional biology. I am comfortable with developmental transcriptomes, lipid and metabolic gene sets, and building interactive tools that make a large dataset explorable. I would be glad to help connect the lab's metabolic questions to the tissue and the timeline where they play out.

Methods note

Data: BrainSpan Atlas of the Developing Human Brain, RNA-seq gene-level RPKM, 524 post-mortem samples spanning 8 weeks post conception to 40 years across multiple brain regions. For each gene, expression is averaged across the regions sampled at each age and shown as log₂(RPKM + 1). Genes with very low expression everywhere are left out of the explorer. The early-childhood window is defined as birth to 8 years. This is a cross-section across ages in different individuals, not a trajectory followed within one child.
